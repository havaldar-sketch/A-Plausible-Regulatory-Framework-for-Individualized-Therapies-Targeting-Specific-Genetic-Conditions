Current Application

Out-of-date Regulatory Frameworks and Standards in an Ever-evolving Industry

Currently, the regulatory frameworks and standards appear out-of-date and in need of refinement for the ever-evolving approach in industry, especially considering the current attempt towards devising individualized therapies targeting specific genetic conditions.

Current regulatory frameworks and standards specify the requirements for assuring the reasonable safety and effectiveness of a product for evaluation and subsequent approval by regulatory agencies.

Additionally, agencies are increasingly utilizing real-world data (RWD) and real-world evidence (RWE) derived from electronic health records, registries, and insurance claims to supplement clinical trial data in their assessment of therapeutic products.

For appropriate adherence to regulatory requirements for identification, investigation, and elimination of root causes of quality deviations or defects, an organization may adopt Corrective and Preventive Actions (CAPA) frameworks.

Implementation of Regulatory Frameworks Noting Recent Advancements

Furthermore, it appears that the FDA Office of Inspections and Investigations (OII) is currently responsible for ensuring the safety of products under its regulatory jurisdiction. One channel for regulatory communication involves the submission of a pre-clinical development plan (IND) to the FDA.

In appropriate alignment with current industry trends and patterns, the European Medicines Agency (EMA) has also shifted its approach towards updating its regulatory framework for advanced therapy medicinal products (ATMPs) to accommodate emerging technologies and approaches in individualized medicine.

Another compliance approach to certainly consider is the parameters critical for unbiased collection, storage, and optimal use of genomic samples and data. During an inspection, regulatory agencies will evaluate the appropriateness of such governance structures and protocols.

Considerations of Relevant Examples for Devising a Plausible Regulatory Framework

The first example to consider involves recent advances in genome sequencing technologies including the rise of the $100 genome, long-read technologies, ultra-high-throughput short-read systems, and single-cell sequencing approaches.

Another recent advancement to consider is the development of the first blood test by researchers at Mayo Clinic and Stanford to map tumor "neighborhoods" for improving the prediction of therapeutic response.

In the context of this paper, a similar approach may be adopted for the development of individualized therapies targeting genetic conditions (non-oncogenic in nature). Noting this example, one must consider how such advancements in diagnostic and therapeutic modalities should be incorporated into regulatory frameworks.
