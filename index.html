A Plausible Regulatory Framework for Individualized Therapies Targeting Genetic Conditions

Shivani Havaldar¹

¹Department of Pharmacy, University of Washington, Seattle, WA, 98195-7630, USA

Summary

The consideration of drafting a plausible regulatory framework for individualized therapies targeting genetic conditions is of immense relevance and significance due to advancements in industry involving the utilization of gene therapy or individualized therapy (eg precision medicine) and its implications for in vivo treatment for genetic disorders. Although certain regulations exist for different forms of therapies (eg drugs), it is extremely crucial to consider the opportunity for unifying pre-existing frameworks while considering the drafting of a plausible regulatory framework for individualized therapies targeting genetic conditions. Certain regulatory challenges identified include the lack of cross-industrial collaboration or information remaining in silos due to reasons unknown, preventing an appropriate regulatory framework and timely real-world implementation for the development, regulatory approval, and administration of individualized therapies targeting genetic conditions. It is certainly plausible that with the development of new approach methodologies or non-clinical assessment models (NAMs), certain aspects of the pre-clinical or non-clinical phases may be concurrent with the clinical phases while considering a plausible regulatory framework for the development, approval, and administration of individualized therapies targeting genetic conditions. In summary, these considerations certainly invite new challenges for the regulatory industry.
