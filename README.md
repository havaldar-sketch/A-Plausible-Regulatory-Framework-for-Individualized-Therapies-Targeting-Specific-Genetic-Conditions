## Table 1: Criteria, Timing & Communication, and Approval Mechanisms for Expedited Programs

| Expedited Program | Criteria | Approval Mechanism | Timing & Communication |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fast Track** | Serious condition; potential to address unmet medical need | Frequent meetings; rolling review (early section submission) | Any time during development; high communication |
| **Breakthrough Therapy** | Serious condition; preliminary clinical evidence of substantial improvement over current therapy | All Fast Track features plus intensive FDA guidance and senior management involvement | No later than end-of-phase 2 meetings; very high communication |
| **Accelerated Approval** | Serious condition; meaningful advantage over available therapy; approved based on surrogate endpoint (refer to intermediate clinical endpoint) | Approval based on proxy markers (for example, lab tests) instead of clinical endpoints; confirmatory trials (Phase III) verify clinical benefit, efficacy, and safety; post-marketing trials (Phase IV) required | Early in development to discuss endpoints; at NDA/BLA submission; standard/high communication |
| **Priority Review** | Serious condition; significant improvement in safety or effectiveness | Shortened review goal of 6 months (as opposed to 10 months for standard review) | At NDA/BLA submission; standard communication |

## Table 2: In-vitro Diagnostics versus New Approach Methodologies or Non-clinical Assessment Models (NAMs)

| Feature | In Vitro Diagnostics (IVDs) | Non-In Vitro Diagnostics (nIVDs) | New Approach Methodologies/ Non-clinical Assessment Models (NAMs) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | Diagnostic procedures performed on biological samples (blood, urine, tissue swabs) that have been removed from the human body. The patient is never in contact with the testing reagents. | Diagnostic procedures performed directly on or inside the living patient. This includes medical imaging, wearable sensors, and diagnostic substances injected directly into the body. | Innovative, human-relevant "tools"—such as organ-on-a-chip platforms, organoids, and AI-driven computational modeling—designed to replace, reduce, or refine animal testing in safety, efficacy, and toxicity assessments. |
| **Primary Purpose** | To detect or diagnose diseases, infections, or conditions in human patients. | To interact directly with the patient or use data/radiation from the body to make clinical conclusions. | To evaluate the safety, toxicity, and efficacy of new drugs or chemicals without animal testing. |
| **What is Tested** | Patient samples (eg blood, urine, saliva, or tissue). | Patient samples (eg heart rhythm, bone fractures, or blood flow). | Standardized biological systems or digital representations (eg human stem cell lines). |
| **Example Technologies** | Home pregnancy tests, COVID-19 PCR kits, blood glucose monitors, and lab-based analyzers. | X-rays, MRI scanners, ultrasound machines, and pacemakers. | Organ-on-a-chip devices, 3D bioprinted tissues, in silico (computer) modeling, and AI-driven toxicity predictions. |
| **Target Audience** | Clinicians, healthcare providers, and patients. | Clinicians, healthcare providers, and patients. | Toxicologists, pharmaceutical researchers, and regulatory agencies. |
| **Regulatory Focus** | Analytical accuracy, clinical validity, and risk-based device classification. | Mechanical safety, software reliability, radiation limits, and biocompatibility. | Human relevance, replacing/reducing animal use, and mechanistic safety insights. |
| **Regulatory Framework** | electronic Submission Template and Resource (eSTAR) Program | electronic Submission Template and Resource (eSTAR) Program | Collection of Alternative Methods for Regulatory Application (CAMERA) |

## Table 7: Regulatory Considerations for Integration of Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources (FHIR) and Digital Imaging and Communication in Medicine (DICOM)

| Feature | Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources (FHIR) | Digital Imaging and Communication in Medicine (DICOM) |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary Focus** | Text-based clinical and administrative records | Medical images, waveforms, and rich metadata |
| **Data Format** | JSON, XML, and RDF | Binary files containing pixel data and a header |
| **System Host** | Electronic Health Records (EHR) systems | Picture Archiving and Communication Systems (PACS) |
| **Core Entity** | Resources (e.g., Patient, Observation, Encounter) | IODs (Information Object Definitions like CT, MR, US) |
| **Protocol** | Native HTTP RESTful APIs (GET, POST, PUT, DELETE) | Traditional TCP C-STORE/C-FIND (and modern DICOMweb) |
| **Payload Size** | Small text files (Kilobytes) | Large binary files (Megabytes to Gigabytes) |
