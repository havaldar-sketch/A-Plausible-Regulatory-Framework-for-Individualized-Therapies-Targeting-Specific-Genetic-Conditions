## International Regulatory Frameworks, Guidelines, and Specifications 

Considerations for international harmonization and implementation involve the International Council for Harmonization of Technical Requirements for Pharmaceuticals for Human Use (ICH) guidelines. These guidelines are categorized into four main areas: Quality, Safety, Efficacy, and Multidisciplinary. In the context of individualized therapies targeting genetic conditions, safety and efficacy are of utmost importance for the development, regulatory approval, and administration of individualized therapies targeting genetic conditions (Table 5). 

Furthermore, frameworks and specifications mentioned by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) are also of relevance while attempting to devise a regulatory framework for individualized therapies targeting genetic conditions (Table 6). Considering the abovementioned guidance documents, ISO/TS 8392:2023 may be particularly worth noting. ISO/TS 8932:2023 is an international technical specification for describing, structuring, and evaluating genomic data used in human genetic detection products and services. ISO/TS 8932:2023 aims to ensure data consistency, interoperability, and quality across the health care and biotechnology sectors. Quality control metrics for DNA sequencing are provided in the standards ISO/TS 22692:2020 and ISO/DIS 22692. Moreover, ISO/TS 20428:2024 defines the data elements and metadata essential for describing structured clinical genomic sequence information in electronic health records, particularly focusing on the genomic data generated by next generation sequencing technology. Although ISO/WD 26125-1 attempts to consider cellular therapeutic products (which may be considered as Advanced Therapy Medicinal Products) and ISO/WD 25430-3 considers endpoint quality assessment of intestinal organoids, a gap remains for defining in vivo therapy or non-clinical assessment models in the context of individualized therapies for genomic disorders.  

One aspect to certainly consider while attempting to formulate a regulatory framework for individualized therapies targeting genetic conditions is the utilization of imaging, ranging from molecular imaging (for drug development) to advanced imaging techniques such as magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and ultrasound. Although the guidance “Medical Device Data Systems, Medical Image Storage Devices, and Medical Image Communications Devices” by the FDA attempts to define the utilization of medical device data systems for medical image storage and communications, ISO 12052:2026 further establishes a Digital Imaging and Communication in Medicine (DICOM) workflow for diagnostic medical imaging and image-based therapy data management. Once again, standards such as ISO/TS 8932:2023 may be necessary for considerations for the management of imaging data as well as likely imaging metadata generated as part of workflows for individualized therapies targeting genetic conditions. This suggestion is made with the consideration of the pre-existing FDA guidance entitled “Use of Public Human Genetic Variant Databases to Support Clinical Validity for Genetic and Genomic-Based In Vitro Diagnostics” as reference.
